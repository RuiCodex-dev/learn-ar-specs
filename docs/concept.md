# WordRoom: concept notes (pre-interview)

Written 2026-10-17, before the `plan-lens` interview. These are the old-idea notes the rules allow;
the binding design is `WordRoom/devpost/design-brief.md`, which the Devpost Learn skill pack writes.

## One line
Learn Dutch words where they live. WordRoom puts the Dutch word, with its article, on the objects
around your kitchen counter. Then it asks you to find them: "Waar is de pan?" You look at the pan
and pinch it.

## Who it is for
Newcomers in Flanders and the Netherlands learning everyday Dutch. In Flanders, civic integration
asks for Dutch at A2 level. The hardest small thing is the article: *de* or *het*. A word list on a
phone teaches the word apart from the thing it names. Glasses can put the word on the thing.

## Why glasses (the three strengths)
- **Draws in the world.** Each tag sits next to its object at real size, a little below eye level,
  within arm's reach. This is the method of loci, spatial memory used as a memory aid.
- **Leaves your body free.** You can keep cooking. One pinch answers; nothing is held.
- **Shares your view.** The quiz asks about what is in front of you, not about a list.

## The one spatial moment
Standing at the counter: a prompt panel asks "Waar is de vork?" The learner looks along the counter
and pinches the fork. It gets a green check and plays the spoken word, and the tag returns.
A wrong pick nudges an arrow toward the right object.

## Article colour
*de* words get one colour and *het* words another, used the same way on every tag. Over time the
colour becomes the memory hook for the article.

## Preview stand-ins
The preview cannot recognise real objects, so the Lens provides a virtual counter with about six
kitchen objects at real scale. On real glasses the same flow would let the learner pinch to place a
tag on a real object. That needs world anchoring only, not object recognition.

## Link to LetsLearn
LetsLearn is Rui Codex's spaced-repetition vocabulary app (TR/NL/EN/DE). WordRoom is its spatial
companion: within a session, words the learner missed come back first. The word list in this repo
is written new for this project. No LetsLearn code or data is reused.
